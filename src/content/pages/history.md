For years, local artists and teachers wanted a gallery of their own. In 1991–92 the South Australian Government was selling buildings in country towns, including Waikerie. This stone villa had been a community centre and was standing empty.

An early committee, including Rob Johnston (Country Arts Officer, Riverland), Lorraine Brock (Year 12 art teacher) and artists such as Ann Rix, lobbied the Waikerie District Council to buy it. The Council agreed by a majority of one vote, setting the rent at $100 a week. The first running committee met in 1992, and the building became the Rain Moth Gallery.

The gallery raised funds through street stalls and quiz nights, and lobbied for a more sensible rent. Since then, volunteers, artists, schools and community groups have shaped the building and its garden:

- **1999:** a large Country Arts SA grant funded a community project: a mosaic wave seat made by students from local schools and community organisations, and a decorative verandah railing.
- **Later:** the front was turned into a wheelchair-accessible paved entrance, which doubles as an outdoor setting for exhibition openings.
- **2005:** the Rain Moth Wing Fountain by Dimitri Nickas of Mildura, funded by the Italian community in memory of those who settled in Waikerie around the 1950s, was placed in the gallery garden by the Council.
- **2009:** the interior was upgraded, and Liz and Clint Frankel made the "Nature's Corridor" glass window.
- **2012:** a Loxton Waikerie District Council Community Grant paid for local metal artist Anthony Leske to make the metal sculpture at the front of the fountain, lit up at night.

The garden project, also called "Nature's Corridor", brought together metal, mosaics and rock: stone plinths, cairns and the river by Michael Black, the sign by Anthony Leske, mosaics by Alan Key, and the river painted by Julie Shaw. Visitors regularly stop to photograph it.

Around the building you'll also find fauna mosaics by Year 5/6/7 Special Interest Art students at Waikerie Primary School (teacher Bronwyn Burford), framed in metal by Richard Hall; mosaic rain moths made by special-needs students at Waikerie High School (teacher Steve Schubert); and Julie Shaw's mural on the fuse box, painting the view from that side of the gallery.
